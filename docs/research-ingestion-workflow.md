# Research Ingestion Workflow

Forming Material follows a capture-first, enrich-later research workflow. A useful source, material, product, Atlas entry, or experiment should be easy to save while the context is fresh, then improved over time by the contributor or an editor.

## Capture First, Enrich Later

Research records can begin as minimal working notes. The goal is to avoid losing useful evidence because the full scholarly record is not ready yet.

Minimum useful research source:

- Title
- Source type
- URL or citation
- Why it matters
- Image when the source is image-led

Minimum useful material:

- Name
- Material family

Minimum useful product:

- Product name
- Material or product category
- Purchase URL when known

Minimum useful equipment:

- Name
- Equipment type

## Research Source Workflow

Use Quick Entry for rapid capture of papers, patents, websites, supplier pages, technical manuals, historical images, process images, videos, and archive objects. Quick Entry focuses on title, type, author, year, URL, DOI, citation, short note, why it matters, and image.

Use Full Record when the source is ready for deeper interpretation: abstract, summary, historical context, forming principle, material relevance, undercut relevance, research translation, tags, and editorial notes.

Duplicate warnings are advisory. If a DOI, URL, or normalized title appears to match an existing source, open the existing record first. Continue anyway only when the source is legitimately distinct.

## Image Provenance

Images may be the primary research value of a record. For historical images, collection objects, museum records, patents, manuals, and supplier material, capture provenance where available:

- Creator
- Original date
- Source archive
- Source URL
- License
- Rights notes or credit line

Do not invent missing provenance. Leave unknown fields blank and enrich later.

## Material To Product To Vendor

Materials describe research-relevant behavior and identity. Products describe exact purchasable or lab-stocked items. Vendors describe the supplier relationship.

When a material page identifies a useful commercial source, create a product from the material page so the material is preselected. When adding a product, select an existing vendor when possible. If the vendor is missing, open Add Vendor in a new tab, save it, then refresh the product form vendor choices.

Approved researchers can use product detail pages for lab reorder decisions. Public visitors must not see internal inventory and reorder information.

## Research To Atlas To Experiment

Research sources capture evidence. Atlas entries translate evidence into reusable concepts: precedent, principle, tool translation, behavior, and strategy. Experiments test those concepts in the lab.

Use Atlas links to connect a source to the principle it supports, the material or tool system it informs, and the experiment where it is tested. Keep the relationship explicit so future researchers can move from evidence to design action without guessing.

## Lineage Vs Related Experiments

Lineage records experimental development:

Parent experiment -> current experiment -> child or next test.

Related experiments are similar work under comparable conditions. They may share a material, forming method, geometry type, or undercut type, but they are not necessarily descendants.

Keep these separate. Lineage explains sequence. Related records support discovery.
